Introduction: The Relevance of Islam in the Modern World

In an era characterized by rapid change, moral ambiguity, and spiritual emptiness, Islam's timeless message offers profound guidance. The core of Islam—submission to the will of Allah—provides a framework for navigating contemporary challenges with clarity and purpose.

Today, as societies grapple with questions of identity, purpose, and morality, Islam's emphasis on inner purification, compassion, and justice speaks directly to the human condition. The faith's holistic approach to life—addressing spiritual, social, and ethical dimensions—offers solutions to modern ills from stress and anxiety to social fragmentation and injustice.

The Quran reminds us that "for without doubt in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find satisfaction" (Surah Raad, Ayat 28). This promise of inner peace is particularly relevant in our fast-paced, anxiety-ridden world.

The Five Pillars of Islam

The Five Pillars form the ritual and practical core of Islam, representing the essential acts of worship that shape a Muslim's life and character:

  1. Shahadah (Declaration of Faith): The testimony that "There is no god but Allah and Muhammad (PBUH) is his messenger." This foundational belief is the gateway to Islam.
  2. Salah (Prayer): Muslims are required to pray five times daily—a practice that cultivates discipline, mindfulness, and connection with Allah.
  3. Zakat (Charitable Giving): Mandatory almsgiving for the poor and needy, teaching generosity and social responsibility.
  4. Sawm (Fasting in Ramadan): Fasting during the month of Ramadan develops self-restraint, empathy for the less fortunate, and spiritual awareness.
  5. Hajj (Pilgrimage to Mecca): The pilgrimage to Mecca, required once in a lifetime for those able, symbolizes unity and submission to Allah.
Note on Purpose: These pillars are not mere mechanical rituals but instruments for developing moral values such as piety, self-restraint, and humility.

How to Follow Sahih Hadith

The Hadith—sayings, actions, and approvals of the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH)—serve as the primary source of Islamic guidance alongside the Quran. Following authentic (Sahih) Hadith requires careful attention to scholarship and methodology.

Key Principles for Following Hadith:

  1. Rely on Authentic Collections: Focus on hadith compiled by renowned scholars with rigorous chains of transmission (isnad).
  2. Understand Context: Consider the historical circumstances and reasons for revelation when interpreting hadith.
  3. Seek Scholarly Guidance: Consult qualified scholars who understand the principles of hadith criticism and jurisprudence.
  4. Balance Quran and Hadith: The Quran is the primary source; hadith explains and elaborates on Quranic teachings.

The Six Authentic Books (Sahih Sittah)

Sunni Islam recognizes six primary collections of authentic hadith, known as Al-Sahih al-Sittah (The Authentic Six):

  1. Sahih al-Bukhari - Compiled by Imam Muhammad al-Bukhari (d. 870 CE)
  2. Sahih Muslim - Compiled by Imam Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj (d. 875 CE)
  3. Sunan Abu Dawood - Compiled by Abu Dawood (d. 888 CE)
  4. Jami at-Tirmidhi - Compiled by Imam al-Tirmidhi (d. 892 CE)
  5. Sunan an-Nasa'i - Compiled by Imam an-Nasa'i
  6. Sunan ibn Majah - Compiled by Ibn Majah

Additional respected collections include Muwatta Malik, Musnad Ahmad, and others.

The Significance of Namaz (Salah)

Salah (Namaz) is the second pillar of Islam and represents the most direct connection between the believer and Allah.

Importance of Salah:

  • Spiritual Nourishment: Salah provides daily spiritual renewal and purification
  • Discipline and Routine: The fixed prayer times structure the day around divine remembrance
  • Humility and Submission: Physical postures in prayer symbolize submission to Allah
  • Community Unity: Congregational prayers strengthen Muslim brotherhood
  • Forgiveness and Guidance: Salah serves as a means of seeking forgiveness and divine guidance

Belief in the Day of Resurrection in Islam

Belief in the Day of Resurrection (Yawm al-Qiyamah) is the fifth article of faith in Islam and is central to the Islamic worldview. It affirms that every soul will be held accountable for its deeds in this life, and that ultimate justice will prevail.

Why This Belief Matters:

  • Moral Accountability: It instills a profound sense of responsibility, knowing that every action—good or bad—will be recorded and judged.
  • Hope and Justice: It assures believers that wrongs will be redressed and that the oppressed will find justice, even if not in this worldly life.
  • Purpose and Direction: It gives life meaning beyond material pursuits, orienting the believer toward eternal success rather than temporary gains.

Quranic Verses on Resurrection:

"And the Trumpet will be blown, and all who are in the heavens and all who are on the earth will fall dead, except those whom Allah wills. Then it will be blown again, and at once they will be standing, looking on." (Surah Az-Zumar, 39:68)

"So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it." (Surah Az-Zalzalah, 99:7-8)

Significance for Daily Life:

  1. Encourages Piety: The belief discourages sin and encourages righteousness even when no one is watching.
  2. Promotes Patience: Believers endure hardships with patience, knowing that eternal reward awaits.
  3. Discourages Arrogance: All humans stand equal before Allah on that Day—no wealth or status will matter.
  4. Fosters Compassion: Awareness of ultimate judgment motivates believers to help others and seek forgiveness.

The Significance of Zikr (Remembrance of Allah)

Zikr (Dhikr)—the remembrance of Allah through repeated phrases, supplications, or reflection—holds immense spiritual significance in Islam.

Importance of Zikr:

  • Spiritual Tranquility: The Quran declares that hearts find satisfaction through the remembrance of Allah (Surah Raad, 13:28)
  • Moral Development: Zikr cultivates qualities like humility, modesty, and prudence
  • Connection with Allah: Regular remembrance strengthens the believer's relationship with the Creator
  • Protection from Sin: Those who remember Allah frequently are more mindful of their actions

Forms of Zikr:

  • Recitation of Allah's Beautiful Names (Asma Al-Husna)
  • Reciting specific phrases like "SubhanAllah," "Alhamdulillah," "La Ilaha Illallah," "Allahu Akbar"
  • Sending salutations upon the Prophet (Salawat/Durood)
  • Reflecting on Quranic verses

25 Prophets Mentioned in the Quran

The Quran explicitly mentions 25 prophets and messengers by name. However, Islamic theology emphasizes that these are not the only prophets God sent; the Quran states that Allah sent a messenger to every nation and that there are many prophets whose stories were not revealed to Prophet Muhammad (PBUH). According to authentic Hadith, there were approximately 124,000 prophets throughout human history.

Key Facts About Prophetic Mentions

  • The Most Mentioned Prophet: Prophet Musa (Moses) is the most frequently mentioned individual in the entire Quran, with his name appearing 136 times.
  • Prophet Muhammad (PBUH): Although he is the final messenger and the recipient of the Quran, his actual name "Muhammad" is mentioned only 4 times. He is also referred to once as "Ahmad" (Surah As-Saf 61:6) and frequently addressed by titles such as "O Prophet" (Ya-Ayyuha an-Nabi) and "O Messenger" (Ya-Ayyuha ar-Rasul).
  • Prophet Isa (Jesus) and Maryam: Prophet Isa is mentioned 25 times. His mother, Maryam (Mary), is the only woman mentioned by her actual name in the Quran, and her name appears more times in the Quran than in the entire New Testament.
  • The Prophets of Strong Will (Ulul 'Azm): Five prophets are given the highest title of Ulul 'Azm (Possessors of Constancy/Strong Will) due to the immense hardships they faced and their unwavering patience. These five are: Nuh (Noah), Ibrahim (Abraham), Musa (Moses), Isa (Jesus), and Muhammad (PBUH) (referenced in Surah Al-Ahqaf 46:35 and Surah Ash-Shura 42:13).
  • The Core Message: Despite being sent to different nations at different times, the Quran emphasizes that every prophet brought the exact same core message: the oneness of God (Tawheed) and the command to worship Him alone (Surah Al-Anbiya 21:25).

The Story of Adam (عليه السلام) (First Human) and the Prostration of Iblees (Devil)

The Announcement

Before the creation of humanity, Allah (سبحانه وتعالى) announced His divine plan to the angels:

"And [mention] when your Lord said to the angels, 'Indeed, I will make upon the earth a khalifah (vicegerent/successive authority).'"
— Surah Al-Baqarah (2:30)

The angels, in their awe and reverence for Allah, asked:

"Will You place upon it one who causes corruption therein and sheds blood, while we declare Your praise and sanctify You?"
— Surah Al-Baqarah (2:30)

Allah replied with absolute wisdom:

"Indeed, I know that which you do not know."
— Surah Al-Baqarah (2:30)

Adam is Taught the Names

Allah created Adam from clay, shaped him with His own hands, and breathed into him of His spirit. Then He honoured Adam with knowledge that even the angels did not possess:

"And He taught Adam the names — all of them. Then He showed them to the angels and said, 'Inform Me of the names of these, if you are truthful.'"
— Surah Al-Baqarah (2:31)

The angels humbly admitted:

"Exalted are You; we have no knowledge except what You have taught us. Indeed, it is You who is the Knowing, the Wise."
— Surah Al-Baqarah (2:32)

Allah then said: "O Adam, inform them of their names." And Adam named them all. The angels realised the honour Allah had bestowed upon this new creation.

The Command to Prostrate

Then came the great test — a command from Allah to all those present:

"And [mention] when We said to the angels, 'Prostrate before Adam,' and they prostrated, except for Iblees."
— Surah Al-Baqarah (2:34)

This command is repeated in multiple places in the Quran, emphasising its gravity:

"So when I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My [created] soul, then fall down to him in prostration."
— Surah Al-Hijr (15:29)

"And We have certainly created you, [O mankind], and given you [human] form. Then We said to the angels, 'Prostrate to Adam'; so they prostrated, except for Iblees. He was not of those who prostrated."
— Surah Al-A'raf (7:11)

All the angels prostrated in obedience. Every single one — except Iblees.

The Arrogance of Iblees

Iblees was not an angel. The Quran clarifies:

"And [mention] when We said to the angels, 'Prostrate to Adam,' and they prostrated, except for Iblees. He was of the jinn and departed from the command of his Lord."
— Surah Al-Kahf (18:50)

He had been elevated among the angels due to his previous worship, but when the command came, his true nature was exposed. Allah asked him directly:

"[Allah] said, 'O Iblees, what prevented you from prostrating to that which I created with My hands? Were you arrogant [then], or were you [already] among the haughty?'"
— Surah Sad (38:75)

Iblees responded with the first act of arrogance in existence:

"He said, 'I am better than him. You created me from fire and created him from clay.'"
— Surah Al-A'raf (7:12)
— Also in Surah Sad (38:76) and Surah Al-Hijr (15:33)

He refused. He was arrogant. He became among the disbelievers.

The Curse and the Challenge

Allah expelled Iblees from His mercy:

"[Allah] said, 'Then get out of Paradise, for indeed, you are expelled. And indeed, upon you is My curse until the Day of Recompense.'"
— Surah Al-Hijr (15:34–35)

But Iblees, rather than repenting, asked for a respite:

"He said, 'My Lord, then reprieve me until the Day they are resurrected.'"
— Surah Al-Hijr (15:36)

Allah granted it:

"[Allah] said, 'So indeed, you are of those reprieved, until the Day of the time well-known.'"
— Surah Al-Hijr (15:37–38)

Then Iblees declared his war against humanity:

"He said, 'By Your might, I will surely mislead them all, except, among them, Your chosen servants.'"
— Surah Sad (38:82–83)

"He said, 'Because You have put me in error, I will surely sit in wait for them on Your straight path. Then I will come to them from before them and from behind them and on their right and on their left, and You will not find most of them grateful.'"
— Surah Al-A'raf (7:16–17)

Adam in Paradise and the Whisper of Iblees

Allah placed Adam and his wife in Paradise and honoured them:

"And We said, 'O Adam, dwell, you and your wife, in Paradise and eat therefrom in [ease and] abundance from wherever you will. But do not approach this tree, lest you be among the wrongdoers.'"
— Surah Al-Baqarah (2:35)

But Iblees, true to his vow, came whispering:

"Then Satan whispered to them to make apparent to them that which was concealed from them of their private parts. He said, 'Your Lord did not forbid you this tree except that you become angels or become of the immortal.'"
— Surah Al-A'raf (7:20)

"And he swore [by Allah] to them, 'Indeed, I am to you among the sincere advisors.'"
— Surah Al-A'raf (7:21)

Adam and his wife were deceived. They ate from the tree:

"So he made them fall, through deception. And when they tasted of the tree, their private parts became apparent to them, and they began to fasten together over themselves from the leaves of Paradise."
— Surah Al-A'raf (7:22)

The Descent to Earth and the Repentance

Allah addressed them:

"He said, 'Did I not forbid you from that tree and tell you that Satan is to you a clear enemy?'"
— Surah Al-A'raf (7:22)

Adam and his wife immediately turned to Allah in sincere repentance. They did not make excuses. They did not blame anyone. They said:

"They said, 'Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers.'"
— Surah Al-A'raf (7:23)

And Allah, the Most Merciful, accepted their repentance:

"Then Adam received from his Lord [some] words, and He accepted his repentance. Indeed, it is He who is the Accepting of repentance, the Merciful."
— Surah Al-Baqarah (2:37)

They were then sent down to Earth — not as a punishment without hope, but as the fulfilment of Allah's original plan:

"We said, 'Go down from it, all of you. And when guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows My guidance — there will be no fear concerning them, nor will they grieve.'"
— Surah Al-Baqarah (2:38)

Reflection: This story is one of the most repeated narratives in the Quran, mentioned in at least seven different Surahs, because it contains the foundational lessons of human existence: obedience, humility, repentance, and the eternal struggle between good and evil.

How to make Shahada (declaration of faith) in Islam

Taking the Shahada (the Islamic declaration of faith) is the first and most important pillar of Islam. It is the act that officially brings a person into the fold of Islam. The process is simple, but it carries profound spiritual weight. Here is a step-by-step guide on how to take the Shahada, the words to say, and the conditions required.

1. The Words of the Shahada

To become a Muslim, you must recite the following declaration of faith out loud.

In Arabic:

أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا ٱللَّٰهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ

Transliteration (How to pronounce it):
“Ash-hadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan ‘abduhu wa rasuluh.”

English Translation:
“I bear witness that there is no deity but God (Allah), and I bear witness that Muhammad is the servant and messenger of God.”

2. The Core Conditions (What is in the Heart)

Simply saying the words like a magic spell is not enough. For the Shahada to be valid and accepted in Islam, it must be accompanied by the following internal conditions:

  • Knowledge (Ilm): Understanding what the words mean (that only God is worthy of worship, and Muhammad is His final messenger).
  • Certainty (Yaqeen): Having absolute faith without doubt.
  • Sincerity (Ikhlas): Saying it purely for the sake of God, not to please people, gain a spouse, or for worldly reasons.
  • Truthfulness (Sidq): Meaning the words genuinely in your heart, not just saying them with your tongue.
  • Submission (Inqiyad): A willingness to submit to God's commandments and live according to Islamic principles.

3. How to Perform the Shahada (The Process)

Step 1: Make the Intention (Niyyah)

In your heart, make the sincere intention that you are taking this step to embrace Islam and submit to the One True God.

Step 2: Say the Words Out Loud

You must pronounce the Shahada out loud. You can do this alone in your room, in nature, or anywhere you feel comfortable. God hears you wherever you are.

Step 3: Witnesses (Highly Recommended)

While your conversion is entirely valid between you and God if you say it alone, it is highly recommended to say it in front of witnesses (usually two Muslims, or at an Islamic center/Mosque).

Why? Having witnesses integrates you into the Muslim community. It allows the community to support you, teach you, and recognize you as a brother or sister in faith (which is necessary later for things like Islamic marriage or burial).

4. What Happens Immediately After?

1. A Clean Slate (Forgiveness of Sins)

In Islamic theology, the moment a person sincerely takes the Shahada, all of their past sins are forgiven. It is as if you were just born. You start your spiritual journey with a completely clean slate.

2. Perform Ghusl (Ritual Bath)

It is a highly recommended Sunnah (practice of the Prophet) for a new Muslim to take a full shower or bath (known as Ghusl) immediately after taking the Shahada.

How to do it: Make the intention to purify yourself for Islam, wash your entire body from head to toe (including rinsing the mouth and nose), ensuring water touches every part of your skin and hair.

3. Learn the Basics of Prayer (Salah)

You are not expected to know everything about Islam on day one. Islam is a journey of learning. However, the most important next step is to learn how to perform the five daily prayers (Salah). You can ask the Muslims who witnessed your Shahada, or a local Imam, to help you learn.
